Chapter One

Making the Most of Holiday Break

Holiday breaks are more than just time off—they're opportunities. Here's how to make them count.

2/17

The Power of Productive Days

Don't waste all your time. Try to get in at least 4 hours of productive work every day during breaks.

Remember: this time is when you have the greatest opportunity—but it's also when you can waste it the most.

Build the discipline of being productive even when no one is watching. It will serve you throughout life.

3/17

Small Habits Compound

Key Insight

Small daily habits compound over time:

  • 20 pages/day = 25-30 books per year
  • 30 min of practice/day = 180+ hours per year

Consistency beats intensity every time.

4/17

Set Your Compass

Make some resolutions for the New Year—and actually try to stick to them.

Develop 3-4 specific goals you want to achieve by end of semester. Write them down.

Keep them visible: on your desk, mirror, or phone wallpaper. Visual reminders keep your aspirations front and center when distractions pull you off course.

5/17

Plan Your Semester and Summer

Make a plan for the upcoming semester and summer. Just making a plan helps guide your thoughts and shows you how to make best use of your time.

Even if plans change, the act of planning forces clarity about what you want.

6/17

Breaking the Social Media Trap

Instagram and TikTok are engineered to be addictive. These platforms employ psychologists and data scientists whose job is to keep you scrolling.

Understand that you're being manipulated.

7/17

Replace Passive with Active

Instead of scrolling, build habits of:

  • Reading books and blogs
  • Listening to podcasts
  • Watching educational YouTube

If you can't break the habit, do what Pratik did—delete the apps. Or set a strict 30-minute daily timer.

8/17

Protect Your Mornings

Try This

Implement a "no phone before 9 AM" rule.

The first hour of your day sets your mental state. Don't let algorithms decide it.

Use mornings for reading, planning, or exercise instead.

Chapter Two

Academics and Career Choices

Your career will take up a huge portion of your life. Here's how to navigate the choices ahead.

10/17

Find Work You Enjoy

The best career choice is something you enjoy doing every day.

But finding that doesn't happen by magic. You have to be intentional and try things.

Intern. Shadow professionals. Do projects. Volunteer. The gap between what you think a job is and what it actually is can be enormous.

11/17

Track Your Interests

Try This

Keep an "interests journal."

When you find yourself naturally curious about something, write it down.

After a few months, patterns will emerge that point toward work you'll genuinely enjoy.

12/17

Start Somewhere

Be deliberate but make a choice. Don't worry if it's the wrong choice—you can always change later.

Then learn deeply about that field. Read about it. Talk to people in it. Understand the sub-areas.

You can't steer a parked car.

13/17

The Skills That Actually Matter

Academic success is important. But these matter more for career success:

  • Problem solving
  • Communication & influence
  • Learning agility
  • Positive attitude

Master these and they'll help way more than any single subject.

14/17

Master AI Now

AI is changing the world. Learn how to use it.

You don't have to be an AI engineer—but if you learn to use AI tools effectively, you'll differentiate yourself in any field.

Reality Check

AI won't replace most jobs, but people who use AI will replace people who don't.

15/17

Build Your Story

When interviewing, people want to hear your story. They won't scrutinize your GPA as long as it's good.

Look for opportunities every day that add to your story.

Practical Tip

After any major experience, write a reflection within 24 hours. These become invaluable for interviews.

16/17

Ask for Help & Build Relationships

You have a fantastic network through our contacts and friends. People are happy to help—but you have to ask.

If someone helps you, follow up. Send thank you notes. Share updates.

Build relationships before you need them. The best networking doesn't feel transactional.

17/17

It's a Marathon, Not a Sprint

You don't need everything figured out right now.

Small, smart decisions made consistently over time lead to remarkable outcomes.

Be thoughtful about your choices, open to learning, and willing to put in consistent effort.
